# 05 — YC Playbook Transfers

> This adds to the existing FOUNDER-FIT shortlist and replaces nothing on it. The goal is to borrow *how* YC companies win, not to compete in *their* industries.

## 1. Method

1. **Source set.** YC batches W25 through F26 (local `data/yc-w25-f26.csv`), plus a few tier-1 VC-backed companies (a16z, Base10, General Catalyst and others) where they define a pattern.
2. **Cluster into playbook archetypes.** Companies are grouped by *mechanism*: what the product does, who it replaces, and how it gets paid. Vertical is ignored at this step. That gives 32 archetypes (section 2).
3. **Record coverage.** For each archetype we list the industries YC and VC companies already serve, so we don't copy them.
4. **Transfer.** Each archetype is applied to traditional industries it has not yet reached (towing, mineral royalties, termite inspection, fuel delivery, freight forwarding, water utilities and so on). The question for each one: does the same structure (documents, counterparty, labor pool, system of record, payment logic) exist there?
5. **Competitor check and AI edge.** Each transfer got a quick web search for competitors in the target industry, where search budget allowed. Rows marked "not searched" are unverified.
6. **Quick score (0–100).** A rubric covering technical fit, no domain credential needed, bootstrap path, product (not services), market size, AI advantage over competitors, and go-to-market without a network. Crowded or unverified ideas were marked down. TAM figures are rough estimates unless a source is cited.

Competitors are acceptable. The bet throughout is that an AI-native product can take share from form-digitizers, portals, consultants and legacy software.

## 4. Advisor's picks and rationale

These 10 cover ten different archetype and industry combinations, so no two overlap. None repeats an idea already on the FOUNDER-FIT list. Where several transfers described much the same opportunity, the strongest version was kept.

1. **HookOS (53), towing and impound system of record.** Starts with a per-state lien-sale and notice engine that runs next to Towbook, so customers don't have to migrate. The incumbents are dispatch tools and the niche is fragmented. Highest quick score.
2. **StubLedger (52), royalty and mineral-owner ledger.** Owners forward check stubs and get a reconciled ledger. Starts as prosumer card revenue, then moves up to mineral funds and O&G CPAs. Each new operator layout helps every owner (a network effect). Fits the founder's B2C instincts. NonOp Audit (#75) is merged in as the upmarket tier.
3. **WDO Scribe (52), termite inspection report scribe.** Self-serve per tech by card. Incumbents digitize the form but make the tech fill it in; this app fills it from voice and photos. Start with the CA WDO or AZ WDIIR form, which may avoid NPMA licensing.
4. **KeepFull (52), propane and heating-oil tank forecasting plus routing.** The wedge is a free audit of last season's delivery CSV. The forecasting and routing are real technical work. Chosen over TankLine and WillCall, which are the same vertical.
5. **ForwardDesk (51), computer-use clerk for small freight forwarders.** Starts with carrier invoice to accrual matching. Raft and Expedock serve larger, CargoWise-heavy forwarders, leaving Magaya users and the long tail open.
6. **SubmittalCheck FP (51), fire sprinkler and alarm submittal checker.** Checks completeness and code against each fire marshal's checklist, on the PDF the fire marshal receives, regardless of CAD tool. The competitors found are design tools. The jurisdiction rule library compounds over time.
7. **OperatorPath (50), water and wastewater operator tutor and copilot.** Starts as consumer exam prep, a channel incumbents don't have, then becomes a per-plant copilot with no integration. Nyad is the only competitor found and is at pre-seed.
8. **PrequalCopilot (50), contractor-side ISNetworld/Avetta prequal autopilot.** The security-questionnaire playbook applied to a large network of SMB subcontractors. Risks: PrequalPilot and the platforms' terms of service.
9. **EPR Ledger (50), packaging EPR compliance autopilot for mid-market brands.** More states going live forces demand, and Assent is built for enterprise. Adjacent to RegistryPilot's Shopify buyer but a separate product. StateShelf (#32) is the alternate if you want distance from RegistryPilot.
10. **LeakLedger (50), AIM Act refrigerant register built from service tickets.** Refrigeration service contractors are a free channel. Competing tools need technician data entry or a controller integration.

**Caveats.** All 10 have competitors where they were searched; the bet is that a better AI-native product takes share. Some higher-scoring ideas were cut for one of four reasons:
- **Near-duplicates of a pick:** TankLine, NonOp Audit, and PayApp Runner vs WaiverChaser.
- **Crowded:** WillCall, TowIntake, UnitLine, InspectVoice, PackQuote, FloorTakeoff, InspectorDraft.
- **Archetype already represented:** Arrange, PayApp Runner, LicenseRun.
- **Services-heavy:** AppealWorks, DepoStack, CoopOps, MEP FDE.

TAM figures are rough estimates unless a source is cited, and rows marked "not searched" need a competitor check before anyone builds on them.
