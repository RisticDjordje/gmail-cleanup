## 2. Cross-cutting patterns

1. **The ranking flips for a product-first technical founder.** Datasets and verification engines rise: Air Headroom (47), contents reconstruction (46), Subcontract Rights Guard (42) and the data-hall readiness engine (40). Contingency desks, roll-ups and licensed-signer plays fall into the 18-30 range. No old idea reaches 70.
2. **In most ideas the moat sits outside software.** In about two thirds of them, the binding asset is a license or seal, an insider relationship, or human sign-off. These score 2-5 on product-not-services no matter how good the AI is.
3. **A durable technical edge comes from turning unstructured public or semi-public documents into a structured asset nobody has.** Examples: permits to parcel headroom, signature cards to beneficiary data, prose sequences to CDL, data exports to inventories. "LLM extracts, rules engine checks" on its own is table stakes.
4. **Bootstrap-to-raise works best with profit-motivated, price-insensitive buyers you can reach without a network.** Examples: developers, mass-tort firms, MEP controllers, signatory contractors. It works worst with banks, carriers and utilities, where TPRM and 9-18 month cycles come before revenue.
5. **Many claimed whitespaces collapsed when checked live** (LTL, dealer warranty, property tax, LIHTC, SPA validation, Medicaid frailty). A funded AI-native in the same lane is close to disqualifying, so verify the competitive set before building.
6. **Payment structure matters more than channel.** Flat SaaS or per-file pricing gives fast, predictable cash. Contingency creates 6-24 month lags and fights over attribution.
7. **Messaging, voice and SMS are never the differentiator.** They are commodity (Vapi templates) and often add TCPA exposure. The edge comes from data assets, verification logic or modeling depth.
8. **Engineering-moat ideas are the most fundable but bootstrap worst** (MGA risk graph, multi-ERP normalization, AI-vs-AI dispute investigation). Ideas that bootstrap well tend to have shallow moats or low ceilings.
9. **The best old shape** is a paid audit or contingency wedge on structured data held by a findable mid-market buyer. Push automation to 85%+ touchless resolution, then raise on the dataset that accumulates across customers.
10. **Repeated blockers:** government RFPs; licensed acts (public adjusting, CAM, NICET/PE, RA, appraisal); incumbents that own the system of record (Verisk, Vantaca, ServiceTrade, CCC); and funded AI-natives that already hold the obvious wedge (Miter, Reserv, Corgi, XBuild, Canals, FireDesign.ai).
11. **A reusable pattern:** an LLM compiles messy documents into versioned rules, a deterministic engine does the math and cites every flag, and outcome data compounds. Aim it at categories with larger recurring software budgets and no incumbent that owns the system of record.
12. **B2C strength rarely transfers directly.** Only the auto diminished-value tool and the self-serve SKU risk report use consumer-style GTM, and both work better as lead magnets than as standalone products. (Round 6 changes this; see §3.)
13. **Old next step (superseded by §4):** validate Air Headroom and contents reconstruction cheaply, with Subcontract Rights Guard as the SaaS fallback.

## 4. Advisor's shortlist rationale

**Selection criteria:** engineering or AI quality is the moat, a smart outsider can start without credentials, paid revenue in 3-6 months on little capital is plausible, and there is a credible fundable story.

**From the new pool (6):**
- **Switchboard (64).** The purest tech-moat idea: extractors, an AI mapper that learns across conversions, and differential verification. Vendors already pay $500-3,000 per conversion, so revenue is immediate. Buyers are findable and no domain expertise is needed.
- **GP Liftoff (63).** The same legacy-exit engine with a dated forcing event (the GP sunset) and a partner channel that pays per report from month 1. Diligence it with Switchboard, and fold in AccessLift and ReportShift as alternative wedges or later modules (shared parity-test engine), rather than spending three slots on one thesis.
- **CodeTab (63).** The closest match to what the founder has actually built (knowledge tracing from his tutor). Self-serve cash within weeks. Open questions: expanding to all licensing exams or workforce credentialing, code copyright, and the free-AI price anchor.
- **RegistryPilot (66).** A forced mandate plus a self-serve Shopify app where his B2C product sense applies. The moat is the network of reused supplier test reports. Verify CPSC enforcement intensity and Comply PRO+.
- **PlanSet (60).** A parametric-CAD and deterministic rules engine is the edge, it had the highest whitespace score (7), and programmatic SEO can bring per-plan revenue. Map state stamp and preparer rules and E&O exposure.
- **FieldSignal.** The deep-ML B2B option with little mid-market AI-native competition. Cold outbound supports $15-30k diagnostics that convert to $50-100k subscriptions. Seed thesis: quality intelligence for mid-market manufacturing.

Self-serve and B2C coverage: CodeTab, PlanSet and RegistryPilot, plus the consumer intake flow in contents reconstruction.

**Old ideas kept (2), reframed as tech-led:**
- **SPA claims engine (55).** A claims-automation product whose kill metric is touchless resolution rate.
- **Total-loss contents reconstruction (46).** A provenance-tagged evidence pipeline sold per claimant to the licensed firm. It is the closest match to his consumer-data and multimodal skills.

**Excluded, and why:**
- Linegraph: Operon has a head start, the accuracy bar is unforgiving, and plants demand on-prem.
- StudyReady: AmpSketch is close, the market is niche, and it needs PE credibility.
- PortalRunner: Monto and Tesorio already sell it.
- PrequalAutopilot: the platforms control the channel.
- TariffWise and CAM Close: unverified whitespace, and contingency or seasonal economics.
- RateCompiler, OpexCheck and DepositProof: crowded.
- GreenScreen: services drift and slow IT buyers.
- Air Headroom: a small cyclical market that needs air-modeling expertise.
- Hard-class MGA: needs an underwriter and fronting.
- DisputeShield: regulated-FI vendor-risk cycles.
