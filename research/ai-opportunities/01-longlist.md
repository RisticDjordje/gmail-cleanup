## Trends & trajectories observed

### construction-trades
- Construction AI is consolidating. Recent acquisitions: Trimble bought Document Crunch (~$250M, Apr 2026), Bluebeam bought Firmus, Clariti bought CivCheck, ServiceTrade bought Mura. Exits in the $100-300M range are now validated, but narrow features have capped standalone upside.
- Takeoff and estimating are commoditized: every CSI division now has a trade-specific AI takeoff tool.
- YC and seed money is concentrated in generic contractor back-office agents. YC has 126 real estate and construction companies.
- Funded category leaders: PermitFlow ($54M B), Adaptive ($30M B), Parspec ($20M A), Trunk Tools ($40M B).
- Data centers are absorbing the construction economy, at a $50-75B annual rate. Starts were $81.5B in 1H26. This is a cyclical bet on AI capex.
- Labor shortages are structural and are moving into white-collar roles (inspectors, commissioning engineers). ABC estimates a 349k shortfall in 2026.
- State law is opening code compliance to AI: FL HB 683, CA SB 379 / SolarAPP+, TX HB 14.
- Federal whiplash: IIJA expired 9/30/26 and the DBE interim final rule suspended certifications.
- 50% Section 232 tariffs on steel, aluminum, and copper are creating a wave of escalation claims.
- PE roll-ups (e.g., Pye-Barker, 57 acquisitions in 2025) are buyers for AI in specialty trades.
- Rollback risk: HFC rule reconsideration, softened building performance standard (BPS) deadlines in CO and Denver.
- Insurance-funded construction is an AI-vs-AI arms race. Citation-grade evidence is worth more than generated narrative.
- Services-as-software is the recurring pattern for small teams.

### insurance
- Casualty claims AI is asymmetric. Plaintiff side has scaled (EvenUp, $2B). Defense side is fragmented. "Claimslop" overload.
- Full-stack AI-enabled services (Strala, Corgi, Harper, Novella) are attracting more funding than pure SaaS.
- Underwriting copilots and submission intake are crowded (Sixfold, Federato, FurtherAI, and others).
- The MGA and fronting boom has created an oversight vacuum: MGA premium ~$128B, fronting ~$30B.
- E&S is softening: premium +7.8% while item count rose +14.1%.
- Claims workforce is shrinking: ~25% retire by 2027, and entry-level postings are down ~50%.
- Regulatory deadlines cluster in 2025-26: Section 111 civil money penalties (CMPs), NAIC Best Interest, GA SB 68, NAIC AI bulletin.
- Workers' comp is profitable but flat at $41.6B, so carriers will chase leakage.
- Agency consolidation: ~37k agencies, 695 M&A deals in 2025. AI is the #2 challenge agencies report.
- Annuity money in motion: $461B in sales, with a 3- to 5-year MYGA surrender wave in 2025-29.
- Exits are strategic acquisitions (DigitalOwl to Datavant, VIPR to Bridgepoint).
- Juries may treat insurer cost-cutting AI as evidence of bad faith. Explainable AI is safer.
- Search notes: some sites were blocked. Estimates are the scout's own.

### logistics
- Regulation is setting the 2026 freight cycle. Spot rate hit a record $3.83/mi, +43% YoY in Q3. Drivers: CDL and English-language-proficiency (ELP) rules.
- Tariff whiplash created a refund economy: IEEPA struck down ($166B), Section 122 voided, Section 301 replacement, CAPE portal, secondary claims market, 21 class actions.
- USMCA claim share rose from ~45% to ~86%, and claims face an audit tail. The DOJ Trade Fraud Task Force has collected $1B+.
- Freight brokerage AI is saturated (HappyRobot unicorn, 50+ platforms).
- Freight audit is moving up-market (Loop $95M C).
- Customs AI seed cohort: Amari, Gaia, Pax, Zollback, Nabu, Glimpse.
- The NMFC density reclass turned freight class into a measurement.
- Cargo crime is shifting to identity fraud (~$725M, +60%).
- The FMC D&D billing rule makes defective invoices disputable. §541.4 was vacated.
- Data-native MGAs (Nirvana, $1.5B) are repricing small-fleet insurance.
- Amazon DSP platform risk ($1.9B investment).
- The de minimis suspension cut parcels 54%. There are ~11k licensed brokers and the number is flat.
- Gaps: rail demurrage and ocean D&D volumes were not sized.
