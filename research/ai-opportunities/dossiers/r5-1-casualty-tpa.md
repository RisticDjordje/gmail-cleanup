# Casualty Severity TPA (R5-1)

**One-liner:** An AI-native, defense-side claims administrator for trucking, commercial-auto and contractor-GL programs. It focuses on the roughly 5% of claims that drive most loss dollars: time-limited demands, reserve adequacy against AI-inflated specials, excess and reinsurance notices, and panel-counsel control. The best route in is to buy a regional casualty TPA and AI-enable it, not to build one from scratch.

## Thesis

US MGA premium is about $128B (Conning, 2025). It has grown double digits five years running, about 20% of it is fronted, and more MGAs now delegate claims handling to TPAs. Casualty is where losses concentrate. Third-party medical specials rose about 33% between Q1 2023 and Q1 2026 (CCC snippet). Plaintiff AI (EvenUp: $385M raised, 700+ firms) now produces policy-limits demands at industrial scale. The defense side's answer is "pay right, pay fast, document good faith," not "pay less."

Reserv's $100M ARR proves that AI-native TPAs work as a business, so it is no longer whitespace. The opening left is specialization: severity-focused casualty handling in the reform-turbulent states (FL, GA, TX, LA, MO), entered through takeover and run-off books and ideally through acquisition.

## Workflow today

- **Contracts.** Program and claims administration agreements set authority limits. The front approves which TPA handles program claims, and that approval is the real gate.
- **Handling.** Claims go to in-house MGA units, incumbents (Sedgwick, Gallagher Bassett, Crawford, CCMSI), regional specialists, or AI-native TPAs (Reserv, Corgi). Adjusters carry 100-175 files each. Demands arrive by mail, fax or email and are often misrouted. Specials go through Colossus, CCC or Enlyte review. Authority goes through roundtables, which is where 14-30 day demand clocks run out.
- **Reporting.** Bespoke monthly Excel bordereaux. Narratives and demands stay inside the TPA system.
- **Oversight.** Fronts and reinsurers sample-audit 25-100 files per year at roughly $15-60K per audit (estimate). Findings arrive months late.
- **Litigation.** On severe claims, defense panel counsel (DCC) spend often exceeds the TPA fee.
- **Churn.** When a program switches fronts or an MGA fails, its open casualty book has to be re-procured under time pressure.

## Wedge and path to scale

- **Entry, Profile C.** Buy a $5-25M-revenue regional casualty TPA with trucking and GL program contracts in FL, GA or TX, at an estimated 5-8x EBITDA.
- **Entry, Profile B.** Win one takeover or run-off book of 2-5K open claims through front relationships.
- **Years 0-1.** Add an AI severity desk: demand-clock detection with dual human verification, reserve-versus-specials checks, excess and reinsurance notice automation, and specials reconciliation of AI-written demands. Use AI to raise adjuster caseload from about 125 toward 175-200 files.
- **Years 2-3 ($10-25M).** TPA on 10-20 programs. Add litigation management and contingency recovery. Optionally run a separately branded neutral scan unit, firewalled from the TPA, sold to fronts as a takeover channel.
- **Years 4-8 ($40-100M+).** Roll up 2-4 more regional TPAs onto the same operating model. Expand into hired/non-owned auto, excess casualty, self-insured fleets and risk retention groups. Sell benchmarking seats to capacity providers.
- **Exit.** To a consolidator, a front or DA platform, or Reserv.

## Steelman summary

The specialist sells outcomes on the claims that cost the most. Next to indemnity plus DCC the TPA fee is a rounding error, so a severity edge supports premium pricing. Reserv proves buyers pay but does not close the market, because fronts approve TPAs program by program and no TPA can credibly audit itself. Services margins are what roll-up buyers pay for. Commoditized audit tooling raises demand for the judgment layer. Program churn is the customer-acquisition channel. Davies, Sedgwick and the live consolidation bid validate the Profile C playbook. Steelman score: C hybrid 66, B 60.

## Skeptic summary

- **Competition and GTM.** "Severity specialist" is positioning, not a moat, and Reserv or Corgi can add a casualty unit faster than a newcomer gets licensed. The neutrality pitch conflicts with the scan-to-takeover funnel, so fronts and incumbents will restrict data access. Takeover books are distressed for a reason. Valuation comps are weak: about 2x revenue, 5-8x EBITDA.
- **Feasibility, regulation and liability.** Near-100% recall on demands forces human double-checks, which means BPO economics. Courts are split on direct TPA bad-faith liability. E&O policies may exclude the insurer's extra-contractual exposure. Texas B-0003-26 and the NAIC AI bulletin (24 states plus DC) require human review and vendor governance. Life-of-claim pricing creates duration and working-capital mismatch. Skeptic scores: C 58-60, B 55, A 45, 0 30.

## What's good

- The market is proven: AI-native TPA economics validated at about $100M ARR, and a delegated-claims pool growing about 12%/yr.
- Severity concentration makes defense-side quality worth paying for, and plaintiff AI keeps raising the stakes.
- Program churn and front switches create recurring, time-pressured procurement events for defined books.
- Under Profile C, every startup barrier (licenses, approvals, adjusters, data, revenue) is bought on day one, and AI cost compression lands directly in EBITDA.
- An active consolidator exit market (Sedgwick, Davies, Intercare, Ryze) gives a credible exit even at services multiples.

## What's bad

- Three funded AI-native TPAs, with trucking and liability already in their positioning, plus incumbents shipping AI.
- Gross margin of 35-50% and about 2x-revenue comps mean this is not a venture return unless it gets re-rated as software.
- Catastrophic tail: one missed policy-limits demand can outweigh years of fees, and E&O cover is uncertain.
- The neutral-scan wedge contradicts the TPA ambition. C should treat the scan as optional or drop it.
- PE competition bids up acquisition targets. Inherited run-off liabilities must be ring-fenced.

## Best founder profile and why

**C, paired with a B-type COO.** Acquisition turns the skeptics' strongest objections (licensing, approved-TPA gate, capital, references) into purchased assets, and turns the services-margin objection into the value-creation lever: buy at 5-8x, lift margins 5-15 points, roll up, sell at platform multiples. B is the runner-up: native access, but slower and capital-constrained. A has to out-specialize a KKR-backed leader with seed money. 0 should not pursue this.

## First 30 days (Profile C/B)

1. **Build the target list.** Use MarshBerry/Axia-style screens and state TPA license registries to list 25-40 regional casualty TPAs with $5-25M revenue and trucking or commercial-auto program contracts in FL, GA and TX. Contact 10 owners about succession.
2. **Run 10 interviews** with front and casualty-reinsurer claims leaders (Accelerant, State National and others). Test whether they would approve a new or acquired TPA, whether they would pay for severity outcomes, and what their actual takeover-book pipeline looks like.
3. **Do an E&O and liability check.** Get two broker quotes for TPA E&O covering extra-contractual exposure. Get counsel memos on direct TPA bad-faith exposure in FL, GA and TX, and on Texas B-0003-26 human-review requirements.
4. **Run a severity prototype.** On a redacted sample of 500 closed litigated files from a friendly MGA or target, measure demand-detection recall, reserve-versus-specials gaps and missed notices. Quantify dollars at stake to set the pilot ROI case.
5. **Line up financing.** Search-fund or independent-sponsor capital plus SBA/senior debt term sheets, built around a 6x-EBITDA platform deal.
