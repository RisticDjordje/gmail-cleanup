# Qualified Occupancy Desk: LIHTC file review, flipped to the investor side

**One-liner:** The original pitch sold LIHTC move-in income certification as an outsourced service paid for by developers. After review, the more defensible version is an AI-first *independent* file-review and pre-8823 surveillance service, paid for by syndicators and bank investors. Investors carry 15 years of compliance risk, and their own review queue is what holds up the stabilization pay-in.

> Research caveat: the shared web-search budget was used up before this verdict, and direct fetches were blocked in every upstream stage. All figures come from training knowledge (cutoff mid-2026) and are **unverified this run**. The OBBBA specifics, HOTMA dates and competitor funding all need checking.

## Revised thesis
Don't be the preparer. Be the reviewer. Syndicators (Enterprise, RBC CI, Hunt, Merchants, Boston Capital, R4, CREA, Raymond James, Red Stone, Cinnaire, OCCH, NEF) and bank direct investors already pay internal teams or consultants to review 100% of initial lease-up files, and samples every year after that. Their queue is what gates the stabilization pay-in. An AI-first reviewer sells three things:
1. 48-hour first-pass review of initial files, with an HCCP-certified human adjudicating every flag.
2. Continuous pre-8823 surveillance that ranks a portfolio by inspection risk before HFA visits under Reg 1.42-5.
3. A defect benchmark across sponsors and fee managers.

Independence is the reason to buy, so there's no conflict to explain away. Recurring revenue follows inspection cycles, not construction schedules. Liability is lower because the service flags problems and the owner still signs the TIC. Yardi and RealPage sell to owners, not investors, so they can't bundle this. The first wedge is **HUD-layered LIHTC/PBRA files**, where the HOTMA rules and Section 42 practice diverge and defect density is highest. Once 3-5 syndicators run on this format, sell sponsors and property managers a cheap "pre-clear against your syndicator's rubric" tool. That brings back the original lease-up speed thesis, now with the gatekeeper's blessing.

## How it is done today
Interest list, then pre-screen, then a questionnaire signed by every adult, then third-party verifications: VOEs by fax or the Work Number, paystubs, 6 months of bank statements, SSA letters, student checks. Verifications go stale after about 120 days, which forces rework. A specialist annualizes income under 4350.3 (plus HOTMA rules if HUD-layered), checks MTSP limits, and executes the TIC. Then a regional QC pass, then 100% syndicator review with kickback loops lasting weeks, then HFA sampling under Reg 1.42-5 with 8823 findings. Each file takes about 4-10 specialist hours and 2-6 weeks elapsed, at a loaded cost of $150-400. Consultant reviews run about $75-250 per file and temp labor about $60-150 per file (all estimates).

## Why now
- OBBBA (July 2025) reportedly makes the 12% allocation increase permanent and lowers the bond test to 25% (verify). That points to bigger and more numerous 4% deals, leasing up from about 2028.
- HOTMA's dual rulebook on HUD-layered deals.
- Compliance labor scarcity.
- Multimodal LLMs can now read paystubs and bank statements and detect tampering.
- Higher bridge-loan carry makes time-to-stabilization more valuable.
- HUD staffing cuts push quality burden onto owners and investors.

## Wedge and business model
**Pivot:** 2-3 syndicators as paying pilots. Start with 100% initial-file first-pass review on HUD-layered and Income Averaging deals at about $60-150/file, then a portfolio pre-inspection subscription. Gross margin starts around 50% with a human on every flag and should reach 70%+ as AI-first triage holds. Seed of about $1.5-3M.

**Original (now secondary):** a developer-paid lease-up file factory at $350-500 per qualified unit. Keep it only as a later "pre-clear" product.

## What's good
- A real, under-appreciated insight: under the Available Unit/140% rule and the HERA recert exemption, the **move-in TIC carries the value**, and an over-income-at-move-in error can't be cured until that household leaves.
- First-year credit shortfalls defer to year 11 under 42(f)(2), so investors price lease-up quality in dollars.
- LLMs fit read-only checklist review of long, messy files very well. Recall matters more than precision when a human adjudicates.
- No confirmed AI-native competitor. The incumbents are a PMS duopoly that sells to owners and small consultancies with no technology.
- A pooled defect dataset across portfolios could become a benchmark no single owner has.

## What's bad (strongest objections, by lens)
- **GTM:** The stabilization pay-in is released on the *later of* 8609s, perm conversion, 90-day DSCR, 100% qualified occupancy and the cost cert. Files are rarely the last gate. The 42(f)(1) deferred-start election and LPA cushions already hedge first-year loss, so the "$30-50k/month saved" ROI is mostly not attributable. The price anchor is temp labor at $60-150/file, not carry cost.
- **Competition:** Lease-up compliance is already paid for inside fee-manager management and lease-up fees. Integrated sponsors (Related, Dominium, Lincoln) do it in-house. The developer buyer is a thin tail of mid-size self-managers. Yardi and RealPage control write-back.
- **Competition / feasibility:** A developer-paid preparer can't be "fast-tracked" by syndicators. Their review is a fiduciary control for bank/CRA investors, so the original moat is structurally contradictory.
- **Feasibility:** Liability is asymmetric (a $400 fee against $50-150k of credits per bad file). That forces a human on every file, plus FCRA "consumer report" risk, Fair Housing disparate-impact risk (SafeRent ~$2.3M settlement, 2024; verify) and Colorado AI Act exposure.
- **Market:** The Sunbelt wedge states (TX/FL/AZ) have a *demand* problem. 60% AMI rents are near concessioned Class B rents, so faster files don't speed lease-up. The OBBBA wave lands after a 2026 seed runs out.
- **Ceiling (all lenses):** The incumbent consultancies have stayed small for decades. That is evidence the outsourced niche is low-margin and relationship-locked. The pivot fixes GTM but narrows the market to about 30 buyers who may prefer in-house teams.

## Non-obvious insights
1. The recert market is smaller than it looks and the move-in moment is worth far more. Price at move-in.
2. The real bottleneck is often the syndicator's review queue and its kickback loop, so the gatekeeper is the right customer.
3. HOTMA vs. Section 42 asset rules ($50k vs. $5k self-cert thresholds) create a growing defect pocket on layered deals that a deterministic engine handles better than tired specialists.
4. Income Averaging means one misdesignated 80% unit can break the project average. That is portfolio-risk monitoring syndicators would pay for.
5. Routing verifications to payroll APIs before the Work Number creates data-cost arbitrage, but only if HFAs accept API data as third-party verification (state by state).
6. Likely acquirers are syndicators, Novogradac/CPA firms and fee-manager roll-ups, not just PMS vendors.

- **Net:** a real problem with real buyers, but the most likely outcome is a $1-5M ARR tool or services shop in a ~30-buyer market with sub-$1/file software competition. That does not fit "few AI-native entrants."

### Pre-mortem: top failure modes (hypothetical narrative: acqui-hired in 2029 at about $1.4M ARR with 48% gross margin)
1. **Buyer pool and ACV ceiling** (~45%). About 30 syndicators plus dozens of banks at $100-300k each caps the business at $5-15M ARR. *Warning sign:* nobody will price a portfolio deal above $150k.
2. **Syndicators won't outsource fiduciary review** (~35%). It gets bought as a seat tool, capped at FTE savings, with 6-12 month procurement.
3. **Price compression** (~30%). Consultancies bolt LLMs onto their HCCP staff. **TenantAudit at $49-99/mo is already evidence of this.**
4. **Owner-side bundling** (~30%). Yardi, RealPage, Pronto and LeaseBase pre-clear files, so defect density falls where we charge.
5. **Data starvation (~20%) or a public miss (~15%).** DPAs bar pooling, and one missed over-income Income Averaging household spreads through a 30-buyer community.
6. **Timing** (~20%). The OBBBA wave and HOTMA land in 2027-29, after the seed runway.
7. **No credentialed co-founder** (~20%).

### Discovery-call script (ask for the last real instance, not a typical one)
1. Walk me through your last lease-up batch: how many files, who reviewed them, and how many days from receipt to sign-off?
2. What were the top 3 kickback reasons? What share of files bounced, and how many rounds did the worst one take?
3. How many reviewer hours go into a clean file versus a HUD-layered or Income Averaging file? Is that tracked or a gut number?
4. During the last volume spike, did you use overtime, a consultant, or a slipped deadline? What did it cost, and who approved it?
5. What was the last surprising 8823 or HFA finding? When did you learn of it, what was the root cause, and what did it cost?
6. Between the initial review and the HFA inspection, what do you monitor, and how do you pick this year's sample properties?
7. Who else reviews the same files? Do you see their findings? When did two reviewers last disagree?
8. Have you tried any software or service for this in the last 2 years (TenantAudit, Pronto, ProLink, PMS AI features)? Why did you keep it or drop it?
9. Where does the overflow or tool budget sit, who signed the last contract, and what vendor-risk review did it pass?
10. If I returned flags on 20-30 of your already-reviewed, redacted files within 48 hours, what result would get a paid pilot? Who else must agree? Ask for files, time or money.
