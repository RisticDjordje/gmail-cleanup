# Desktop-First AI-Native Environmental Due Diligence for CRE Lenders

**One-liner:** A licensed, AI-enabled environmental consultancy. AI reconstructs parcel history from Sanborn maps, aerials, topo maps, city directories and state file rooms, and a credentialed Environmental Professional (EP) reviews and signs. It starts with desktop screens for SBA and bank lenders and moves up to ASTM E1527-21 Phase I reports.

## Revised thesis

As pitched, this is a cash-generating services firm capped at roughly $15-30M of revenue. It would fight industrialized national firms (Partner, AEI, BV, Terracon) for a $300-600 per-report labor gap, inside a channel owned by its own data supplier. The best version stops selling borrower-paid transaction reports and **sells to whoever bears the cost directly**:

1. **Bank ERM / credit-review copilot.** It ingests every vendor Phase I and screen, checks it against E1527-21 and the bank's own policy, flags missed adjoining-property or file-review issues, and drafts the bank's internal environmental memo. The bank pays from opex, because review labor is its own cost. No EP signature, panel spot or E&O on the report is needed.
2. **Back-book re-screening.** Quarterly diffs of agency databases and AI land-use history (with PFAS-likely past uses flagged) across existing loan collateral, servicer portfolios and pollution-liability insurer portfolios. Only flagged parcels are routed to partner EPs.

Both build the evidence-linked REC calibration dataset, which is the only asset that could reach software margins and insurer underwriting. A licensed Phase I firm stays an option for later, via acquisition once there is cash flow and lender relationships. Caveat: both buyer pools are niche (a few hundred banks with dedicated ERM staff, plus a few dozen environmental insurers and MGAs), so even the pivot is likely sub-venture unless the insurer data layer works.

## How the work is done today

The lender orders through Collateral360 or by email to a panel firm. The firm buys a radius report and a historical package from EDR, ERIS or GeoSearch ($250-700 est., 1-3 days) and files FOIA requests or pulls state file rooms (1-30+ days, which is the real critical path). A 1099 field assessor visits the site ($250-500 est.). An analyst spends 6-16 hours drafting a 40-200 page report, and the EP reviews it for 1-3 hours (40 CFR 312.10 "responsible charge"). The bank's ERM desk then reviews it again. Elapsed time is 10-15 business days, and a 5-day rush costs 25-50% more. At a $2,000-2,500 panel price, gross margin is about 30-45%. Smaller loans get database screens, ASTM E1528 transaction screens, desktop reviews or SBA RSRAs at $300-900.

## Why now

ASTM E1527-21 (recognized under AAI around 2023) raised the expected historical review, including for adjoining properties. The PFOA/PFOS CERCLA designation (2024) widened scope. Vision LLMs can now read Sanborn sheets and scanned agency PDFs. The 2025-27 CRE maturity wall and record SBA 7(a) volume drive orders. Investors now accept AI-enabled services roll-ups.

## Wedge and business model (original)

A 48-hour, EP-signed desktop screen (RSRA, desktop review, E2600 vapor screen, Phase I updates) at $400-900, then 5-day Phase Is in CA and FL. Target 55-65% gross margin. First customers would be 4-5 SBA preferred lenders, 3-4 community banks and 1-2 net-lease or data-center acquirers. An optional accelerant is buying a retiring owner's consultancy at 3-6x EBITDA.

## What's good

- AI genuinely compresses the most tedious work: historical land-use reconstruction and boilerplate drafting.
- The EP-signature requirement keeps pure software out of the full Phase I tier.
- The bank's own review time is a real, unpriced cost, and structured, evidence-linked output addresses it.
- Phase I updates (180-day and 1-year) are high-margin and near-free with a current parcel graph.
- Online state file rooms (GeoTracker, EnviroStor, OCULUS, DataMiner) remove FOIA waits in launch states.
- The EP bar is lower than assumed: a bachelor's degree plus 5 years of experience qualifies under 312.10.

## What's bad (red-team, by lens)

- **Competition:** The desktop wedge competes with the data vendors' own near-zero-cost automated screens, sold through their own channel. An EP signature may add cost without adding required value if SOP 50 10 8 RSRA needs no EP. Nationals have already captured most of the labor arbitrage, so the AI edge against them is about $200-400 per report.
- **GTM:** The borrower pays, so a lower price does not move the lender. Turnaround is rarely the last item before closing (prior: under 15% of loans). Bank TPRM (2023 interagency guidance) asks for years in business, E&O history, footprint coverage and AI model-risk answers. A community bank yields $15-80k a year at 25-35% panel share, against $20-40k CAC. Bank consolidation churns panels. The acquisition path turns the company into a leveraged PE roll-up.
- **Feasibility:** Misses cluster in exactly the cases where vision LLMs are weakest (1950s directory names, Sanborn paste-overs, low-resolution aerials), so the EP still has to look at every source. Realistic throughput gain is 1.5-2x, which means 40-50% gross margin, not 55-65%. "AI drafted it and the EP reviewed for 20 minutes" is a discovery attack on AAI compliance itself. EDR license terms may forbid building a derived parcel graph. ASTM E50, where incumbents sit, could require explicit per-source review.

## Non-obvious insights

1. The "free historical data" claim fails where it matters. LoC Sanborns are mostly pre-1925, while 1930s-60s editions and certified no-coverage findings sit with EDR. The data cost per Phase I drops 20-40%, not to zero.
2. The data vendor, not the consultancy, is the real competitor in the desktop tier, and it is also the supplier and the channel.
3. The cost the bank actually bears is internal review labor, so a review copilot sells into a budget that report vendors never touch.
4. Nobody re-screens the back book. Collateral Phase Is go stale after a year while PFAS and new database listings keep arriving. That gap is a recurring, non-cyclical product.
5. Liability caps (fee-limited engagement letters) make "our AI catches more RECs" economically weak for the buyer, which leaves speed as the only selling point, and speed rarely matters.
6. Pick launch states by online file-room quality, not by market size.

## Cheapest validation test (2 weeks, under $1k)

- Run 15 structured calls with lender ERM and credit officers through Environmental Bankers Association contacts and LinkedIn. Ask three questions: (a) how often environmental is the last item before closing; (b) hours spent reviewing each vendor report and the comment-round rate; (c) whether they would pay per loan for a review copilot or a back-book re-screen.
- In parallel, read the SOP 50 10 8 environmental appendix to see whether an RSRA needs an EP, request ERIS and GeoSearch price sheets and license terms for derived data, and run a one-day Crunchbase/YC/LinkedIn sweep for AI Phase I entrants and LightBox AI announcements.
- Kill if under 3 of 15 cite review labor as a top-3 pain, or if LightBox already ships AI review or drafting.
- Bonus: back-test a vision LLM on 20 public Sanborn sheets with known gas-station or dry-cleaner uses (about $50 of API spend).
